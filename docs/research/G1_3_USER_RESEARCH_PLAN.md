# RoadShield DZ — G1.3 User Research Plan

## Objective
Validate the problem from the perspective of the people who experience, report, maintain, or manage road-condition risk.

This phase does **not** assume that RoadShield is already the correct solution.

## Research questions

1. How do drivers currently learn about road hazards and degraded road segments?
2. How often do they encounter hazards that are not clearly represented in current maps or reporting channels?
3. What do they do after encountering a pothole, damaged surface, obstruction, missing signage, flooding, or another road hazard?
4. How much trust do they place in existing road-condition information?
5. What makes a road-risk report useful enough to act on?
6. For fleets, which road-condition signals affect route planning, vehicle wear, delivery reliability, or driver safety?
7. For road operators/local authorities, how are road-condition observations collected, verified, prioritized, and closed after maintenance?
8. Would repeated observations from independent trips be more useful than a single unverified report?
9. What evidence is required before a road-condition event can be considered actionable?
10. Which RoadShield output would create measurable value: driver alert, evidence map, maintenance priority, fleet intelligence, or another workflow?

## Target participant groups

### Group A — Regular drivers
Target: 8–12 participants.

Include a mix of:
- daily commuters;
- long-distance drivers;
- taxi/VTC drivers;
- drivers who travel between wilayas where practical.

### Group B — Professional/fleet users
Target: 5–8 participants.

Include where accessible:
- delivery drivers;
- logistics operators;
- transport companies;
- fleet supervisors.

### Group C — Road/maintenance stakeholders
Target: 3–5 participants where access is feasible.

Potential roles:
- municipal technical staff;
- road maintenance contractors;
- infrastructure/road operators;
- transport or road-safety professionals.

Total target: 16–25 interviews.

## Interview method

Use 15–20 minute semi-structured interviews.

Do not begin by pitching RoadShield.

Start with recent real experiences and current workflows.

### Interview sequence

1. Context: driving frequency and route type.
2. Recent incident: "Tell me about the last road problem you encountered."
3. Discovery: "How did you know about it?"
4. Action: "What did you do next?"
5. Pain: "What was difficult or missing?"
6. Current tools: maps, reports, WhatsApp/groups, local knowledge, official channels, etc.
7. Trust: "What would make you trust a road-condition signal?"
8. Verification: "Would repeated independent observations matter to you? Why?"
9. Value: "Who would benefit most from better road-condition evidence?"
10. Only at the end: show the RoadShield concept and ask for reaction.

## Avoid leading questions

Do not ask:
- "Would you use RoadShield?"
- "Isn't it useful to know about potholes?"
- "Do you like our idea?"

Instead ask:
- "How do you solve this today?"
- "What happened the last time?"
- "What information was missing?"
- "What would have changed your decision?"

## Evidence capture

For each interview record only sanitized research metadata:

- participant group;
- approximate driving context;
- problem mentioned;
- current workaround;
- frequency estimate as stated by participant;
- trust requirement;
- willingness-to-use signal;
- willingness-to-pay signal where relevant;
- notable quote, with consent and anonymization;
- researcher interpretation separated from participant statement.

Never commit names, phone numbers, addresses, exact home/work locations, or unrelated personal data.

## Hypotheses to test

### H1 — Road-condition information gap
Drivers frequently encounter road-condition problems that are not sufficiently visible, current, or trusted in existing tools.

### H2 — Verification gap
A single user report is often insufficient for trusted operational action; repeated independent observations could improve confidence.

### H3 — Operational value
Road-condition evidence can be useful beyond navigation, especially for fleet operators and maintenance stakeholders.

### H4 — Low-friction sensing
Passive smartphone telemetry can produce useful road-condition evidence without requiring the driver to manually report every event.

### H5 — Current-state value
The usefulness of an event depends on knowing whether the condition is new, persistent, improving, resolved, or stale.

## Success thresholds

These are research thresholds, not market claims:

- At least 60% of interviewed regular drivers report a recent road-condition problem that was difficult to discover, communicate, or verify using their normal tools.
- At least 50% describe a workaround or information gap that is materially inconvenient or risky.
- At least 50% of relevant professional/operations participants identify a use case for verified road-condition evidence beyond consumer navigation.
- At least 50% of participants exposed to the concept consider repeated independent observations meaningful for trust.

Failure is acceptable.

If thresholds are not met, revise the problem, target user, or product proposition before Phase 2.

## Deliverables

1. Sanitized interview log.
2. Affinity map / coded themes.
3. Hypothesis scorecard.
4. Problem statement revision.
5. Target user decision.
6. Value proposition decision.
7. Open questions.

## Gate condition

G1.3 is complete only after the results are documented and the team records one explicit decision:

- **Proceed** — evidence supports the current problem/user hypothesis;
- **Narrow** — evidence supports a smaller, more specific segment/use case;
- **Revise** — evidence contradicts a core assumption.

No Architecture Gate decision should rely on imagined user validation.
