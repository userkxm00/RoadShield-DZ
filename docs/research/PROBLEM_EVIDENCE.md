# RoadShield DZ — Problem Evidence & Assumptions

## Confirmed external facts

### Road safety is a current national priority
In January 2026, the Ministry of Interior presented a road-code reform that included modern control mechanisms and national information systems for monitoring road accidents and identifying black spots, within a broader prevention and road-safety strategy. (Source: Ministry of Interior, 7 January 2026.)

### Official crash-data system exists
SNAR is described by the Ministry of Interior as a national system for collecting and consolidating bodily-injury crash data from security services and producing statistical indicators for decision-makers. (Source: Ministry of Interior SNAR page.)

### Road maintenance is active nationally
A major maintenance operation for motorway and national-road networks was launched in January 2026 with the stated aim of facilitating transport and ensuring road-user safety. (Source: APS, 10 January 2026.)

### Smartphone road-surface sensing is technically plausible
Peer-reviewed research has demonstrated road-surface monitoring using smartphone GPS and accelerometer signals, including crowdsensing and geospatial localization. Other studies explicitly identify device, vehicle, phone-position and speed variability as important sources of performance degradation. (Examples: ScienceDirect and MDPI studies reviewed for Gate 1.)

## Core problem statement
Road users encounter physical-road anomalies during ordinary travel, but there is a gap between a one-off driving experience and structured, reusable road-condition evidence. Existing consumer navigation systems are primarily designed around navigation and temporary user reports; official national systems focus on recorded incidents and official data sources. RoadShield proposes a complementary sensing layer that turns repeated journey observations into explainable road-segment evidence.

## User pain hypotheses
These are hypotheses, not yet measured claims:
- Drivers cannot reliably know which road segments have persistent surface hazards before reaching them.
- Fleet operators lack low-cost, continuously collected road-condition evidence tied to their actual journeys.
- Road-maintenance prioritization can benefit from additional field evidence between formal inspections.
- Manual hazard reporting is intermittent and subjective.

## What must be validated before competition claims
1. Whether the target users actually experience this gap frequently enough to value the product.
2. Whether smartphone motion signals remain useful across realistic device placements and vehicle types.
3. Whether repeated independent observations materially improve confidence.
4. Whether the resulting evidence is useful to at least one operational stakeholder.
5. Whether the prototype can demonstrate reproducible results on controlled routes.
