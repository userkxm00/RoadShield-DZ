# RoadShield DZ — G1.1 Problem Evidence Baseline

**Phase:** 1 — Research & Innovation
**Status:** Evidence baseline established; primary-user validation still required.

## 1. Research question

Is there a sufficiently important Algerian road-safety and road-condition information problem that justifies a new preventative, field-sourced intelligence layer?

## 2. What is already proven

### 2.1 Road-safety burden is substantial

The DGSN reported **842 deaths and more than 23,000 injuries in urban road crashes during 2025**. citeturn358227search1turn358227search3

Protection Civile weekly national reports during 2026 also show a persistent burden. For example, **54 people died and 2,145 were injured in 1,667 road crashes from 2–8 August 2026**. citeturn358227search7

A separate week, 24–30 May 2026, recorded **49 deaths and 2,214 injuries in 1,772 crashes**. citeturn358227search4

These sources establish the seriousness and continuity of the road-safety problem, but they do **not** prove that road-surface defects are the dominant cause of crashes. RoadShield must not make that claim without further evidence.

### 2.2 Algeria is already investing in road-safety data infrastructure

The Ministry of Interior describes SNAR as a national system intended to consolidate road-crash data, improve reporting quality and timeliness, integrate spatial analysis, and use data-mining methods for analysis and prediction. citeturn358227search0turn358227search6

The Ministry also states that the road-code reform introduces national information systems for monitoring road crashes and identifying black spots. citeturn358227search14turn358227search15

This makes road-safety intelligence a strategically relevant national domain, but also means RoadShield must be differentiated from official crash/black-spot systems.

## 3. The precise RoadShield problem hypothesis

> **During ordinary trips, useful signals about road-condition anomalies are generated continuously by smartphones, but those signals are not automatically converted into privacy-conscious, corroborated, geospatial evidence that can help identify and prioritize road-condition issues before they become part of an official crash record.**

This is a **hypothesis**, not yet a proven market fact.

## 4. What we must NOT claim yet

- We must not claim potholes are the principal cause of Algerian crashes.
- We must not claim drivers universally lack reporting tools.
- We must not claim municipalities currently have no road-condition data.
- We must not claim RoadShield is the first product to detect road hazards automatically.
- We must not claim accident prediction or guaranteed accident prevention.

## 5. Evidence gap that requires primary validation

We need direct evidence from potential users and operators on:

1. How drivers currently notice and report road hazards.
2. Whether reporting is too slow, inconvenient, fragmented, or unreliable.
3. Whether road-condition information becomes stale quickly.
4. How fleet operators currently record road-related incidents and vehicle impacts.
5. How maintenance/road operators receive, triage, verify, and prioritize road-condition complaints.
6. Whether repeated independent observations would be useful to them.
7. Whether an evidence/confidence score would improve prioritization.
8. What data-sharing/privacy constraints would prevent adoption.

## 6. G1.1 conclusion

**Decision:** Continue research. The national problem domain is clearly material and strategically relevant, but the specific RoadShield product problem requires primary-user validation before we present it as a proven market gap.

### Next evidence target

- 10–15 short driver interviews.
- 3–5 fleet/operator interviews where access is practical.
- 2–3 road-maintenance/municipal stakeholder interviews if access is available.
- A controlled sensor feasibility experiment.

The results must be recorded as evidence, not opinions, and incorporated into the Gate 1 decision.
